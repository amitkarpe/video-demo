# Two reference treatments

Both start from the Issue #14 independent creative review (motion-led 2D vector; one problem / mechanism / payoff arc;
one representative slice; motion that explains, not decorates; separate answers for execution, readback, compliance and freshness).
No other pilot's code, animation or narration was read or copied.

## Treatment 1 — "One map, one ladder" (BUILT)

- **Hook:** a healthy-looking approved request that simply goes silent: the acknowledgement signal breaks up at the SDK boundary.
- **Mechanism:** one continuous system map (trusted backend ▸ SDK boundary ▸ mock S3). The camera pushes into the ledger to show
  `CALLING` was saved *before* the call, then follows a read-only probe along its own rail on the same run.
- **Payoff:** the map dissolves into a four-row evidence ladder. Only `PROVIDER READBACK` turns green; red connectors show that
  MATCH does not upgrade execution, compliance or freshness. A `NEEDS_REVIEW` stamp ends it.
- **Why:** it matches the review's "one slice, no slide stack" advice and makes the key distinction visible, not only spoken.
  The deliberate 1.1 s silence after "The reply never arrives." carries the uncertainty.

## Treatment 2 — "Two worlds" split timeline (NOT BUILT)

- **Hook:** the screen splits in two the moment the reply is lost: World A (policy applied) and World B (policy not applied), both looking identical from the backend.
- **Mechanism:** the ledger sits on the seam, reading `CALLING` for both worlds; a retry packet is shown colliding with itself in World A (double write) to justify "do not resend".
  The read-only probe then collapses the split into World A.
- **Payoff:** four stacked gauges (execution, readback, compliance, freshness) with only readback moving.
- **Why not chosen:** it dramatises the retry hazard well, but it implies the system "knows" the world after readback,
  which risks overstating execution; and a fork metaphor takes longer to read at 720p. Kept as a possible A/B revision.
